A broad warranty headline can sound reassuring until a project team needs to understand what it actually covers. ALG publishes product-specific warranty terms because a decorative lamp, a commercial fixture, and an industrial high-bay do not share the same duty cycle or operating conditions.

## The Problem with Blanket Claims

A single warranty number applied across unlike products can hide the details that matter. Application, environmental exposure, expected operating hours, and product construction all affect the level of coverage that is appropriate for a fixture.

Commercial and industrial specifications require a more useful question: what does the current warranty say for this exact product and intended application? A data sheet should make that answer visible before a product is specified.

## Product-Specific Terms

ALG's approach is documentation-first. Warranty information is published on product data sheets so a specifier, distributor, or contractor can review the relevant term with the rest of the product information.

This means decorative lamp collections may have different coverage from industrial high-bay or washdown vapor-tight fixtures. The difference is not a marketing distinction. It reflects the operating environment and the design requirements of the product.

## Published Warranty Tiers

The current warranty framework describes four common tiers: three-year coverage for lighter-duty applications, five-year coverage for general commercial products, seven-year coverage for industrial-grade products, and ten-year coverage for the most demanding continuous-duty environments.

## Verify Before Release

Before a project is finalized, pull the current data sheet for the exact product or SKU. Confirm warranty language alongside listings, certifications, mounting requirements, and other project-specific details. If a figure is unclear or documentation is missing, ask ALG to confirm it before the specification is locked.
